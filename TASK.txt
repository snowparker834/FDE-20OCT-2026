What you're building

Atlas Industrial inherited a chaotic data room of vendor contracts — PDFs, Word docs, a low-quality scan, a forwarded email, a legacy spreadsheet. Analysts clear it by hand today: open each document, hand-key fields into the ERP, eyeball the risk, email Legal for anything unusual. Slow, error-prone, no record of why any decision was made.

Replace it with an agentic system, and prove the improvement with KPIs. The inputs are messy, the output format is strict, the policy is non-negotiable, and the integration is live. How you get from one to the other is your call.

Three things that catch people out, so they're worth saying plainly:

The mock ERP and the schemas are fixed. Don't edit, wrap, proxy or patch them. They're checksum-verified and the judges run your work against the same API. This is the one rule with an automatic consequence.
Keep a build trail as you go. How you specced the problem, how you drove your AI tooling, where it got things wrong and how you steered it. It's scored, the finalist interview covers the same ground, and reasoning we can't see we can't credit. Mostly it's just far easier to write down in the moment than to reconstruct in week four. If you work in git, leave the .gitfolder in your zip — useful corroboration, not required.
Reserve the last three days for the architecture note, the KPI summary and the demo recording. People routinely under-budget the write-up and lose marks on work they genuinely did.
Full detail is in the pack. The attached one-pager is the whole thing on a single page — print it, pin it.
